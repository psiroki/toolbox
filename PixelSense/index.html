Visual Acuity, Contrast Sensitivity & Font Legibility Rules

• Font Height Definition ("Mg" Bounding Box): Calculated font heights represent total glyph bounding box height in pixels—measured from the lowest point of a descender to the highest point of an ascender or capital letter (e.g. the full vertical span of "Mg").

• Minimum Legible Font Height: Requires a total glyph angle of at least 10' arcminutes (yielding a stroke angle of ≈ 1.25' for standard typography). Text smaller than this in pixel count cannot be reliably recognized without severe strain.

• Comfortably Readable Font Height: Requires a total glyph angle of at least 16' arcminutes (stroke angle ≈ 2.0'), allowing effortless continuous reading without eyestrain.

• High-Contrast Acuity Threshold (1 Arcminute): Standard 20/20 visual limit for 100% contrast black/white details. Single pixel strokes under ~1.2' cannot be resolved by the human eye regardless of font size.

• Low-Contrast Spatial Resolution (8-Level Dither): In an 8-level dither, adjacent step transitions have low contrast (ΔC ≈ 14.3%). Due to the human Contrast Sensitivity Function (CSF), low-contrast features require 2.5× to 3× larger angular size (≈ 2.5' – 3.0' per pixel) to be recognized. Dither pixels below 2.5' blend into a seamless continuous tone.
